## Epic A (#868): a cycle's gate answers in minutes

Goal: in a cycle, a gate run takes under 8 min at the median and under 15 min
at p90, and no cycle stops on `verify-timeout` while three cycles run side by
side.

Parts, in order. 1 and 2 first: every later cycle runs on them.

1. **The same tree is checked once.** `scripts/verify.sh` records a pass
   under a key made of the tree (`git write-tree` of a temporary index that
   holds the uncommitted and untracked files), the mode (`--changed`, plain,
   `--full`) and the toolchain versions. The same key again prints
   `ok    cached pass of <tree>` per step and exits 0. A failure is never
   cached. The record lives in the worktree's git directory, so it is never
   shared between checkouts or hosts. The developer, base-health and
   consolidated runs of one cycle often check the same tree: each repeat
   costs nothing. *Built by a person*: `scripts/verify.sh` is a gate (AGENTS.md
   rule 7).
2. **Side by side, the gates do not oversubscribe the host.** Each cycle's
   container may use every CPU but one (`lib/slice.sh`, on purpose: a gate
   alone gets the whole host). So with three cycles, `scripts/test.sh` reads
   a quota of 15 in each and runs up to 45 test files on 16 CPUs. Its `busy`
   check holds new files only above a load of 1.5 times the host's CPUs (24
   here); #803 saw a load of 21. System time (fork and exec) is already 74%
   of the suite, and it grows with contention. Hold new files at a load of
   the host's CPUs instead, and measure the gate's wall time with three
   cycles before and after. No change to the container. This is the
   factory's half of #803. Issue #869.
3. **Fewer processes per dispatch test case: #736.** The dispatch tests are
   40% of the suite's CPU at about 290 processes a case, and the same jq and
   cat calls run in every poll. Done on 2026-10-27: one `jq` reads a store
   answer instead of one per field, the stub `timeout` execs the stub it bounds
   instead of forking a bound around it, and the small `cat` reads of the
   helpers are bash reads. The idle poll went from 405 shim-counted processes to
   339 and its ceiling from 480 to 405; `test/dispatch_park_test.sh` went from
   16.2 s CPU (6.9 user, 9.3 system) to 16.6 s (6.9 user, 9.7 system), which is
   the same figure within the noise of a run, so the `jq` that a poll pays for
   is the poll's own shaping, not the store's shape check.
4. **Gate minutes on the record.** `tehasbot cycle` records each verify run's
   tier and milliseconds from `stream.jsonl` on the run's analytics row, and
   `analytics report` gains a line: gate minutes per cycle (median, p90) and
   gate runs per cycle. Rust, in `console/analytics/`. Issue #870. Without it the goal
   above is counted by hand.

Out of this epic: the driver's half of #803 (one timed-out tier per step,
name the live suite in a hang report) is pi-rukas's. File it there; do not
work around it here.

## Epic B (#730, finish it): an idle tehas costs its host almost nothing

#730's parts 1 to 3 are shipped (#731, #732, #733), and the pinned idle-poll
test (#745). What is left:

1. **Health warns on an over-budget poll: #744.** Labelled `tehas:ready`.
   Without it the next regression goes unseen again, as the 4x one did.
2. **Take out what the idle poll does after its decisions.** #871,
   through `tehas:plan`. Trace a `--dry-run` poll as a child (strace attach
   is refused on the host), count `execve` per step, and remove the top
   three costs. Done when the journal's `Consumed` lines on tehas-tehas,
   idle, are under 3 s CPU and 20 s wall, quoted before and after.
3. **One sandbox image for all instance users of a host: #735.** About
   3.4 GB of layers saved per extra instance on an industry host, and the
   build time of a second user's image.

#736 counts for both epics. It is listed once, under A.
